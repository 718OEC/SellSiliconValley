History Rhymes: The AI Parallel

Today's AI boom (or bubble) feels eerily similar to the Dot-Com surge of 1999/2000. Just like then, we are seeing immense wealth creation. But even when the Dot-Com bubble burst in 2001, home prices merely paused and didn't crash until the unrelated subprime bubble and crisis years later.

The Lesson: Market crashes usually aren't a good time to buy simply because it's in a moment of economic uncertainty.

Market Retrospective

2000
The Dot-Com Peak

NASDAQ hit its all-time high, fueling immense wealth and home buying.

2001
The Dot-Com Bust

The tech bubble burst. Stock portfolios vanished, and home prices took a hit as the local economy contracted.

2008
The Great Recession

Foreclosures spiked. Prices bottomed out in early 2009 at ~$500k.

This $500k bottom cleared the deck for the massive 2012 recovery.
2021
Free money

Interest rates hit historic lows (sub-3%). Buyers had infinite purchasing power, driving the steepest appreciation curve in history.

2022
The Rate Shock

The Fed tripled rates. Prices corrected sharply in Q3/Q4.

2026 Strategy

The "Winter Floor" Trade

Prices are lowest right now. Buying now means fewer choices, but a better price. You are trading "choice" for "value".

Prep for Peak

Do not list in January. Use this month to paint and stage. Hit the market in the spring for maximum ROI.
