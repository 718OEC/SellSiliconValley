monitoring The Climb: Median Sales Price

The decade began in the shadow of the 2008 financial crisis, with prices stagnant at roughly $543k. By 2018, the typical home in Silicon Valley was selling for over $1.5 Million. This chart illustrates the relentless "up and to the right" trajectory that created massive wealth for homeowners.

timer The "Need for Speed": Days on Market

In 2010, you had nearly two months to make a decision on a home. By 2015, the median time a home spent on the market had collapsed to just 7 days. This chart visualizes the moment the market shifted from "Buyers' Market" to "Extreme Sellers' Market."

bar_chart Sales Volume: The Supply Constraint

Notice the blue spikes. Volume peaked in the early recovery years (2011-2013) as distressed inventory cleared. By the end of the decade, despite higher prices, the number of sales began to shrink—a precursor to the "Lock-In Effect" we see today.

history_edu A Retrospective: What Happened?

Timeline
2012

The Facebook IPO

The first major "Tech IPO" of the new era minted thousands of millionaires overnight, kickstarting the luxury market recovery.

2015

The $1M Floor

By 2015, the median house price crossed $1M for the first time. Bidding wars became the new standard as Google and Apple announced massive campus expansions.

2017

SALT Cap Impact

The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act capped SALT deductions at $10k. This effectively increased the cost of ownership, causing a momentary pause in the luxury sector.

2018

The "Rate Spook"

Interest rates touched 5% for the first time in years. Combined with high prices, this caused the first real correction of the decade in late 2018.

Takeaway

The "Golden Window"

Looking back, 2010-2012 was the "Golden Window" of affordability. High inventory and low prices offered a rare opportunity.

The lesson? Don't wait for the perfect moment. Those who bought in the "uncertain" times of 2011 saw their equity double by 2017.
